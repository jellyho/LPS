Read the abstract

Offline reinforcement learning (RL) allows robots to learn from offline datasets without risky exploration. Yet, offline RL's performance often hinges on a brittle trade-off between (1) return maximization, which can push policies outside the dataset support, and (2) behavioral constraints, which typically require sensitive hyperparameter tuning. Latent steering offers a structural way to stay within the dataset support during RL, but existing offline adaptations commonly approximate action values using latent-space critics learned via indirect distillation, which can lose information and hinder convergence. We propose Latent Policy Steering (LPS), which enables high-fidelity latent policy improvement by backpropagating original-action-space Q-gradients through a differentiable one-step MeanFlow policy to update a latent-action-space actor. By eliminating proxy latent critics, LPS allows an original-action-space critic to guide end-to-end latent-space optimization, while the one-step MeanFlow policy serves as a behavior-constrained generative prior. This decoupling yields a robust method that works out-of-the-box with minimal tuning. Across OGBench and real-world robotic tasks, LPS achieves state-of-the-art performance and consistently outperforms behavioral cloning and strong latent steering baselines.

Limitations of Prior Works

Behavior-regularized offline RL (e.g., TD3+BC) hinges on a sensitive weight α. Latent steering (e.g., DSRL) avoids α, but relies on a distilled latent critic whose gradients can be inaccurate.

Behavioral constraint is too sensitive

Large α is too conservative, small α leaves the data support, and the best value shifts with reward scale, data, and model size.

Latent-space critic is not enough

A distilled latent Q can fit values well and still give gradients ∇zQ that point the wrong way.

Latent Policy Steering (LPS)

LPS keeps a fixed generative policy to define the data support and trains a latent actor to steer it, using gradients of the original action-space critic: no latent critic, no α.

1. Differentiable Base Policy via MeanFlow

A one-step MeanFlow policy is differentiable end to end, so critic gradients reach the latent actor directly.

2. Spherical Latent Geometry

Latents live on the sphere Sd−1 (radius √d), which prevents norm blow-up.

3. Direct Latent Policy Steering

ℒLPS = −𝔼s∼D[Qθ(s, πβ(s, πϕ(s)))]

Gradients from Qθ propagate through πβ to πϕ — no proxy latent critic, no α tuning needed.

Experiments

All methods share the same Q-Chunking critic and differ only in how the policy is extracted: QC-FQL / QC-MFQL (action-space distillation), DSRL (latent critic), and CFGRL (classifier-free guidance).

Real-World (Franka)

Four DROID tasks on a Franka Research 3, 50 demonstrations each. LPS averages 56.2%, ahead of DSRL (35.0%), Flow-BC (31.2%), and MF-BC (28.7%).

Simulation (OGBench)

Five state-based OGBench domains plus visual variants. LPS has the highest average, clearly beats QC-FQL and QC-MFQL, and is on par with or better than DSRL in every domain.

Robustness to α

QC-MFQL works only near one task-specific α. LPS stays flat across α and above DSRL at every value.

Computational Efficiency

MeanFlow produces an action in one forward pass, so LPS trains and acts faster than DSRL without backpropagating through an ODE solver.

Ablation Study

  • Sphere latents (a–c): free for the baselines (a) but essential for LPS (b); normal latents blow up and truncated ones saturate (c).
  • One-step MeanFlow (d): beats a 10-step flow (unstable backprop through sampling) and a 1-step flow (inaccurate).
  • Action reformulation (e): predicting denoised actions instead of raw velocity is what makes MeanFlow trainable here.
